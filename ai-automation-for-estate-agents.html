AI automation for estate and letting agents

Estate and letting agents lose hours every week to admin that has nothing to do with selling or letting property: chasing documents, updating portals, re-keying the same details, rebuilding the same report every month, and keeping a chain moving. AI automation can take a lot of that off your team, so they spend more time with clients and less time on paperwork.

By Rashid, founder of Layerform System Ltd. I build real property software.

The admin that eats an estate agency's week

It is rarely one big thing. It is a hundred small ones:

  • Chasing referencing documents, IDs, and signed paperwork.
  • Re-keying the same property and client details across your CRM and the portals.
  • Updating listings and sending the same status updates to vendors and buyers.
  • Sorting and replying to a flood of portal enquiries, most of which look the same.
  • Keeping a sale or let moving by chasing solicitors, references, and the chain.
  • Rebuilding the same vendor, landlord, and management report by hand every month.
  • Watching renewal dates, compliance certificates, and deposit deadlines so none of them slip.

What AI automation can do for an estate agency

Here is where AI and automation genuinely help, in plain terms:

  • Read and file documents. Pull the details out of referencing forms, IDs, and contracts automatically, and flag anything missing.
  • Chase on autopilot. Follow up on missing documents, references, and approvals on a schedule, without anyone remembering to.
  • Sort enquiries. Triage portal leads, spot the serious ones, and route them to the right negotiator.
  • Draft the routine messages. Prepare vendor updates and standard replies for a person to check and send.
  • Keep your systems in sync. Stop the same details being typed into your CRM and the portals separately.

The reporting nobody puts on the list

When agencies tell me where the time goes, reporting is almost never the first answer. It is usually the biggest one.

Somebody in the office spends the end of every month pulling numbers out of the CRM, out of the accounts package, and out of two spreadsheets, then rebuilding the same vendor summary, landlord statement, or management report that they built last month. The layout does not change. Only the numbers do. That is work a computer should be doing.

If the underlying figures already exist somewhere, a report can be assembled and sent on a schedule instead. The same applies at portfolio level: how each branch or each block is performing, where income is drifting, what is coming up for renewal. Those questions get answered once a month because answering them is a day of work. When the answer is generated automatically, they get answered whenever you want to ask.

This is the part of property software I have built the most of, and it is usually where an agency sees the change first, because a whole day comes back every month and nobody had counted it as a job.

Lettings is a different job from sales

Most advice about automating an agency is written about sales, and then applied to lettings, where it does not really fit. Sales is a burst of intense work that ends at completion. Lettings is a long, low, continuous drip of dates and obligations that never ends, and the admin reflects that.

  • Referencing and right to rent. Collecting the documents, reading what is on them, and noticing what has not arrived.
  • Compliance dates. Gas safety, EICR, and EPC expiry dates sitting in a spreadsheet that only gets checked when somebody remembers.
  • Renewals. Tenancies that need a decision weeks before they end, and get one days before instead.
  • Deposits. Protection deadlines and prescribed information, where being late is a legal problem rather than an inconvenience.
  • Arrears and contractor jobs. The chasing that happens between the interesting parts of the job.

Nearly all of that is date-driven, which is the easiest kind of work to automate well. A system that knows every date and tells the right person at the right time removes an entire category of mistake, and it is a much smaller build than people expect.

Sales progression, and chasing the conveyancing

Between the memorandum of sale and completion, an agent is mostly a chaser. Searches, enquiries, mortgage offers, the other side's solicitor, and everyone in the chain above and below. None of it is difficult. All of it is time, and it is time spent on the phone finding out that nothing has changed since Tuesday.

To be clear about what is and is not on the table here: I cannot automate conveyancing, and nobody selling you software should say they can. The legal work belongs to the conveyancer. What can be taken off your team is the machinery around it.

  • The chasing rhythm, so follow-ups go out on a schedule rather than when someone gets to the bottom of the list.
  • A single running log of where every sale has actually got to, instead of the answer living in one negotiator's head.
  • The update to your vendor and buyer, drafted from that log, so they stop ringing to ask.
  • An alert when a case has gone quiet for longer than it should have, which is usually the first sign a sale is in trouble.

Where a person stays in the loop

Some of this should not be automated end to end, and I would rather say so upfront than have you find out later.

Anything that goes out under your name to a client gets drafted automatically and sent by a person. Valuations, offers, and negotiation stay with your team, because that is the job and it is what the client is paying you for. Anything with a legal or compliance consequence, deposits and right to rent in particular, gets prompted and tracked automatically but confirmed by someone who is accountable for it.

The useful version of this is not a system that acts on its own. It is a system that does the preparation and the remembering, and puts a decision in front of the right person with everything already gathered.

Common questions

Will this work with my CRM and the portals I already use?

That is usually the whole point. Most of the time saving comes from connecting the tools you already use, your CRM, inbox, and the portals, so the same details are not typed in three times. I build around your existing setup rather than asking you to switch.

Can you automate our vendor and landlord reporting?

Yes, and it is usually one of the easier wins. If the numbers already exist somewhere, in your CRM, your accounts package, or a spreadsheet, a report can be assembled and sent on a schedule instead of someone rebuilding it by hand every month. Reporting is the part of property admin I have built the most of.

What can you actually automate about conveyancing? We are not the solicitor.

Not the legal work, and I would not pretend otherwise. What can be automated is the chasing around it: the follow-ups to solicitors and the other side, a running log of where each sale has got to, and the update that goes out to your vendor and buyer so they stop ringing to ask. The legal steps stay with the conveyancer.

Do you have experience building software for property?

Yes. I built and run Estate Revenue Manager, a rota and revenue management system for high-end residential property firms. So you are working with someone who has shipped real software in the property world, not just talked about it.

Is this only for big agencies?

No. Smaller agencies often feel the admin most, because there are fewer people to absorb it. You can start with one job, like chasing referencing documents or updating listings, and grow from there.

Yes. I am based in London and work with London agencies closely, and with agencies across the UK remotely.
