How much does AI automation cost?

The honest answer is: it depends on the job. A small automation and a full internal system are very different sizes of work. Rather than make up a number, here is exactly what drives the cost, how it is usually priced, and how to tell if it is worth it.

By Rashid, founder of Layerform System Ltd, an AI automation consultant in London.

The honest short answer

There is no fixed price, because no two businesses waste time in the same way. A small, single automation is a modest one-off project. A custom internal system for a whole team is a larger investment, more like bringing in a contractor for a few weeks. The right way to get a real figure is to talk about your actual work, not to read a number plucked from a web page. The first call is free. After that there is a consultation fee for the session where we go through your processes properly, and you come out of that with a written plan and one fixed price for building it, yours to keep whatever you decide to do next.

What actually drives the cost

Five things decide whether a project is small or large:

  • Scope. One repetitive task is cheap. Re-building how a whole team works is not.
  • Complexity. A clear, predictable process is quick. One with lots of exceptions and judgement calls takes more.
  • Integrations. Connecting to the tools you already use (your CRM, inbox, spreadsheets) adds work, but it is usually where the biggest time savings come from.
  • Data. If your information is tidy, things move fast. If it is messy or spread across many places, tidying it is part of the job.
  • One-off or ongoing. A build-and-hand-over costs less than something I keep improving for you over time.

How AI automation is usually priced

Two common models:

  • Per project. A fixed quote for an agreed piece of work. This is how I prefer to work, because you know the cost up front and there are no surprises.
  • Retainer. A monthly fee for ongoing work. Useful if you want continuous improvement, but you should not pay one just to keep something running.

I quote per project, not on a retainer you do not need. As set out above, that one fixed price comes out of the scoping session, so it is based on your actual work rather than a guess made before anyone has looked at it.

Small automation vs a full system

It helps to think in two buckets. A small automation, like reading incoming documents or chasing missing paperwork, can often be live in days and is a modest cost. A full internal system for a whole team is a bigger investment that pays back over a longer period. You do not have to choose the big version on day one.

How to tell if it is worth it

Forget the price tag for a second and look at the cost of not automating. Add up the hours your team spends on the repetitive task, every week, all year. Multiply by what that time is worth. For a lot of businesses, a single repetitive job quietly costs more in wasted hours each year than automating it would cost once. If it does, it is worth it. If it does not, I will tell you straight.

What a first project usually looks like

Almost nobody should start with a full system. The first project that makes sense is normally one process, for one team, with a clear start and finish. Something like: the documents that arrive by email get read and filed automatically, or the weekly report that someone assembles by hand gets built without them, or the follow-up that keeps getting forgotten gets sent on time every time.

A job that size has three things going for it. It is small enough to price with confidence, because there is not much to guess at. It is quick enough to be live before anyone loses interest. And it is contained enough that if it does not save what you hoped, you find out cheaply. The bigger systems on my homepage are what the far end of that road looks like. Nobody needs to start there.

Four costs that are not in the build price

The build price is the number everyone asks about, but it is not the only one. Four others are worth knowing before you agree to anything, whoever you work with:

  • Running costs. AI services charge per use, and anything that runs all the time needs somewhere to run. For a typical business automation these are small, but they are not zero, and they should be written down before you start. I put them in the plan.
  • Your own team's time. Someone on your side has to explain how the process really works, answer questions during the build, and test the result on real work. That is a few hours, not a few weeks, but it is the part most people do not plan for.
  • Tidying the data. If the information the automation needs lives in three spreadsheets that do not agree with each other, sorting that out is part of the job. It is better to know that at the planning stage than to discover it halfway through.
  • Changes after launch. The first week of real use always turns up something nobody thought of. A support period after launch is included in my fixed price, and what it covers and how long it runs is agreed in the plan, so this is not a surprise bill later.

Where the money goes, step by step

Because the fair question is not just "how much" but "when, and for what", here is the order things happen in when you work with me:

  1. A free first call. Thirty minutes to talk about the job that wastes the most time. If automation is not the right answer, I say so here and it costs you nothing.
  2. A paid planning session. The consultation fee is agreed before we book it. This is where we go through the process properly, look at the tools and the data, and work out what to build and what to leave alone.
  3. A written plan and one fixed price. That is what the planning session produces, and it is yours to keep whatever you decide next. You can take it to someone else, build it yourself, or put it in a drawer.
  4. A small deposit, then milestones. If you go ahead, a deposit starts the work and the rest is paid as agreed pieces are delivered and shown working. No hourly billing, so the risk of it taking longer than expected sits with me, not you.
  5. Launch, with support included. The agreed support period runs after the system goes live, covered by the fixed price.

I do not publish a price list, and that is deliberate. A number on a web page is either so wide it tells you nothing or so specific it is wrong for your business. The planning session exists so that the one figure you get is based on your actual work.

Common questions

Is AI automation cheaper than hiring someone?

Often, yes, for the right task. A person doing the same repetitive job costs a salary every year, indefinitely. Automating that job is usually a one-off build plus small running costs. The honest answer depends on how many hours the task eats and how often. That is exactly what a discovery call works out.

Do you charge a monthly retainer?

I quote per project, not a retainer you do not need. Some clients want ongoing support or improvements after launch, and that can be arranged, but you are not locked into a monthly fee just to keep the lights on.

Are there ongoing running costs?

Usually some, and they are normally small. AI tools and hosting have running costs, but for most business automations these are modest compared to the time saved. I am upfront about any running costs before we start, so there are no surprises.

Can I start small to keep costs down?

Yes, and I usually recommend it. Start with one repetitive job that wastes the most time, prove it saves time and money, then build out from there. It keeps the first cost low and lets the savings fund the next step.

Is AI automation affordable for a small business?

It can be, if you pick the right first job. A small business does not need a full system on day one. One automation that removes the single most repetitive task is a modest one-off cost, and because it is small, the saving is easy to see. If that first piece does not pay for itself, you stop there, and you have lost very little. If it does, it funds the next one.

Why is there no price list on this page?

Because any figure I put here would be a guess about a business I have not seen. The same automation can be a small job for one company and a large one for another, depending on how tidy the data is and how many tools it has to talk to. Instead, the first call is free, the planning session has a fee agreed up front, and that session gives you one fixed price for your actual work. That number is real. A number on a web page would not be.
